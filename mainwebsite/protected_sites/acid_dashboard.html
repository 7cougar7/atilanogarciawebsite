Five rules that protect your barrier

Start at twice a week. Not daily. You can always go up at week 4 — you can't undo a stripped barrier quickly.
Add one new thing at a time, two weeks apart. If you start everything at once and your skin reacts, you'll have no idea which product did it.
Never an acid and a retinoid on the same night. If you add a retinoid later, it goes on the nights the BHA isn't.
Check what you already own. Cleansers, toners and "brightening" serums often contain acids. Most people are already exfoliating more than they think.
Stinging, tightness or flaking means stop. Not push through. Drop back to cleanser + moisturiser + SPF for two weeks, then restart at half the frequency.
Breaking out at first? If it's in your usual spots, started within a week, and the spots clear faster than normal — that's purging. Keep going, it settles in 4–6 weeks. If it's in new areas or the spots won't heal, that's a reaction. Stop the product.
When to judge it. Texture usually shifts at 4–8 weeks. Comedones take longer. Don't change anything before 12 weeks — and take a photo now, because memory is unreliable. No change by then means see a dermatologist; chin-only congestion is often hormonal.
Two things could change this answer. If your skin stings easily, or if spots leave dark marks that last weeks, or if you're pregnant — the recommendation shifts.

Combinations that actually get used, and what for

Salicylic acid + Niacinamide

The workhorse for oily, congested, comedone-prone skin. BHA clears the pore lining; niacinamide reduces sebum output and rebuilds the barrier the acid just stressed. The "these two cancel out at low pH" claim is an outdated myth from unstable 1990s formulas — modern products are buffered. If you're cautious, leave ~30 min between them or alternate days.

Azelaic acid + Niacinamide

Post-acne marks and redness with active congestion. Azelaic is comedolytic and anti-inflammatory and a tyrosinase inhibitor, so it hits the spot and the mark it leaves behind. Very low irritation risk together — this pairing is often used every single day.

AHA/BHA + Hyaluronic acid

Universal safety net. HA is a humectant, pH-neutral, and interacts with nothing. Apply it on damp skin after the acid and seal with moisturiser. There is no version of this routine where HA is a bad idea.

Mandelic acid + PHA

The sensitive-skin exfoliation stack. Both are large molecules that work at the surface only; PHA additionally acts as a humectant and antioxidant. This is what you fall back to if salicylic acid turns out to be too much.

Retinoid + AHA/BHA — alternate nights

Both are effective, but stacked in one night they're a reliable route to a stripped barrier. Acids also drop pH enough to destabilise the retinoid. Standard fix: acid one night, retinoid the next. Or acid in the AM, retinoid PM.

Retinoid + Vitamin C — split AM/PM

Layered together, vitamin C's acidity interferes with retinoid conversion and the retinoid pushes the vitamin C toward oxidising. Neither is dangerous, both just get less effective. Vitamin C in the morning, retinoid at night.

Signs you've over-exfoliated — stop immediately if you see these. Tightness, stinging from products that never stung before, flaking or a waxy shine, new sensitivity, redness, and — counterintuitively — more breakouts. Mechanistically you've thinned the stratum corneum, raised transepidermal water loss, disturbed the skin microbiome and kicked off low-grade chronic inflammation. Recovery protocol: drop all acids and retinoids, use a bland cleanser, a ceramide/niacinamide moisturiser and daily SPF for 2–4 weeks. Reintroduce at half the previous frequency.
Non-negotiable: daily SPF. Every AHA and PHA increases UV sensitivity by removing surface corneocytes. Exfoliating without sunscreen actively works against the texture and tone improvement you're after — and drives post-inflammatory pigmentation from the very comedones you're treating.
The single most common mistake. Not the choice of acid — the stacking. Recommendation: chin comedones + texture

Start here — calibrate to your skin

Three questions only you can answer. They change which exfoliant you should start with, so answer them before reading the rest of this tab.
Why the default answer is salicylic acid (BHA) 2%. Closed comedones are plugs of sebum and dead cells sitting inside the follicle. AHAs are water-soluble and work on the skin's surface; salicylic acid is oil-soluble, so it travels down into the sebum-filled pore where the actual problem is. This is why comparative peel trials consistently show salicylic acid outperforming AHAs specifically on comedone counts. It's also anti-inflammatory, which the chin — a hormonally driven, easily inflamed zone — benefits from. The calibrator above will override this if your skin is reactive or prone to dark marks.

The stack, in priority order

1 · Salicylic acid 2%
Leave-on liquid or gel exfoliant
Core

Your exfoliant. Start 2× a week at night, on the chin and any other congested areas. Build to 3× only if week 4 arrives with zero irritation. Leave-on beats a cleanser — contact time matters.

2 · Azelaic acid 10%
OTC; 15–20% by prescription
Core

A primary comedolytic — it stops comedones forming rather than only clearing existing ones — plus it fades the brown marks they leave. Gentle enough for daily or twice-daily use and doesn't count against your exfoliation budget. Expect 12–24 weeks for a visible verdict. Note the 15% and 20% strengths are the ones with RCT evidence; 10% is the reasonable OTC entry point.

3 · Adapalene 0.1%
OTC retinoid — the strongest option here
Consider

If comedones are the main complaint, a retinoid is the most evidence-backed thing you can buy without a prescription. It's also the reason to expect a purge. Do not start this at the same time as the salicylic acid — add it around week 6–8, only on nights you're not using the BHA.

4 · Niacinamide 4–5%
Every day, AM or PM
Support

Not an exfoliant — the barrier insurance that makes the exfoliants survivable. 2% has been shown to reduce sebum excretion rate at 4 weeks; 4% reduced mast-cell inflammatory infiltrate in a split-face study; it also boosts ceramide synthesis and lowers water loss. Stay in the 4–5% range — 10% products buy irritation, not results.

5 · Hyaluronic acid + moisturiser
Every day, non-negotiable
Support

HA on damp skin, then a ceramide moisturiser to seal it. HA is purely a humectant — without an occlusive layer over it, in dry air it can pull moisture out of your skin. Never skip the moisturiser step.

6 · Broad-spectrum SPF 30+
Every morning
Support

Mandatory once you're exfoliating. Skipping it undoes the texture work and darkens post-acne marks.

If salicylic acid irritates you — switch to mandelic acid 5–10%, not glycolic. Mandelic is the largest AHA, so it penetrates slowest and is gentlest, and it outperformed salicylic on inflamed lesions with markedly less redness. Glycolic is the smallest and harshest of the AHAs and is the wrong first move on already-irritated skin.

12-week ramp-up — one change at a time

The entire point of this schedule is that you only ever add one variable at a time. If something goes wrong, you'll know exactly what caused it.

How to read the results. Give it 12 weeks before judging. Texture usually shifts first (4–8 weeks); comedone counts lag behind. Azelaic acid in particular is documented as taking 12–24 weeks for full effect. If there's genuinely no improvement at 12 weeks, or things worsen after weeks 8–10, that's the signal to see a dermatologist — prescription retinoids or hormonal treatment are the next tier and chin-specific congestion is often hormonally driven.

Purging or breaking out?

Purging is real but over-invoked. It happens when an ingredient speeds cell turnover and pushes existing microcomedones to the surface early — so it only applies to actives that accelerate turnover (retinoids, AHA, BHA), and only in areas you already break out. Answer four questions:

A hydrating serum cannot cause a purge. If breakouts follow a moisturiser, oil, sunscreen or hyaluronic acid serum, that's comedogenicity or irritation — there's no turnover mechanism to purge through. Stop the product.

Barrier protection rules

  • One new active at a time, minimum two weeks apart. Simultaneous introductions make problems undiagnosable.
  • Start at the lowest frequency, not the highest. Twice a week for a month tells you far more than daily for four days.
  • Patch test on the jawline for 3–5 days before applying anything to the full face.
  • Never exfoliate on compromised skin. Stinging, tightness or visible redness means wait.
  • Buffer if needed — moisturiser first, then the active, blunts the impact while you build tolerance.
  • Audit for hidden acids. Cleansers, toners, "brightening" serums and pads routinely contain them.
  • Skip acids after any professional treatment — peels, lasers, microneedling — for at least a week.
  • SPF daily, without exceptions.
